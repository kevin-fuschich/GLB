The Club

The Sultans play baseball like a coastal ritual—slow heat, sudden noise, and a quiet confidence that feels inherited. They win with timing and contact, turning late innings into a street parade. In Mayagüez, the game doesn’t end at the final out; it dissolves into the night.

  • Style: Contact / Timing
  • Vibe: Coastal Ritual
  • Tradition: “The Ninth-Inning Lanterns”

Ballpark

The Salt Court at golden hour

The Salt Court. A waterfront park with warm concrete, painted rails, and a breeze that carries sea-spray into the stands. At sunset, the lights come on like a cue—fans rise, drums pick up, and the late innings feel inevitable.

Broadcast

Broadcast booth with open-window coastal light

Marcos & Lila

Voices of the Sultans. A booth that feels like a porch: open windows, warm light, a little chaos on the desk. They call the game with local rhythm—part baseball, part neighborhood update, always in motion.

Mascot & Stands

Sultans mascot interacting with fans
Mayaguez fans in warm coastal light

El Sultán

A beloved, slightly scuffed costume with real-history energy—more community icon than character. He lives at the rail: high-fives, photos, and the occasional theatrical bow when the crowd gets loud.

The Lantern Section

Families, drums, flags, and a shared superstition: the night turns when the Sultans decide it turns. The section has its own rhythm—constant, communal, and just a little magical.
