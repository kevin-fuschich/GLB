The Club

The Monterrey Cementeros play with the calm pressure of a jobsite that never stops. Nothing wasted, nothing decorative. They build innings the way you set forms—square, tight, and ready for weight. Their wins feel poured, not sparked: patient at-bats, hard contact, and defense that holds its line until the game gives in.

Ballpark

The Kiln Grounds. A concrete-and-steel park framed by industrial light and distant hills. The air is dry, the sound carries, and the game feels close—bat cracks snap sharply and the infield chatter echoes off hard surfaces. Night games run cool and exact.

Broadcast & Announcers

Iván Soria

Play-by-play. Steady, methodical, and precise. Soria calls the game like a foreman reading measurements—clean counts, clean pace, no extra noise.

Rafaela “Raf” Quiñones

In-stadium announcer. Clear and commanding, with timing that lands like a mallet tap. Lineups, counts, substitutions—delivered cleanly through the bowl without a syllable wasted.

Mascot & Stands

Cemento

A heavy, old-school mascot built like a block—more presence than performance. Cemento doesn’t do routines. He patrols the rails, nods at kids, and “sets” near the dugout when the late innings tighten.

The Mix Line

Fans here watch like they’re checking a level—quiet until it matters, then all at once. They clap in bursts, chant in short waves, and lean forward when the game turns heavy. Late applause means it held.
