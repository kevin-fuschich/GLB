The Cementeros

Monterrey is built on heavy industry, limestone, and heat. The Cementeros reflect the ethos of the city’s foundries: unglamorous, structurally sound, relentless. They do not rely on trickery or speed. They play a heavy, compressed game designed to grind opponents into dust over nine innings. To wear grey and slate is to accept that the game is not played; it is worked.

  • Style: Heavy contact / Attrition
  • Vibe: Industrial Noise
  • Tradition: “The 5 PM Whistle”

Ballpark

Estadio Fundidora

Capacity: 18,000–22,000

Carved into the footprint of an old steel mill, the park is known for vertical retaining walls and the “Blast Furnace” in deep center— a seating section that traps midday heat. The surface plays hard and fast, favoring ground-ball pitchers and gap hitters.

Broadcast

Héctor “El Arquitecto” Vega

Voice of the Cementeros

Vibe: Clinical. Rhythmic. Deeply technical.

Vega treats the broadcast like a construction project. He builds the narrative inning by inning, focused on structural integrity and defensive load-bearing plays. Signature sign-off: “The work is finished. The foundation holds.”

Mascot & The Stands

Cimiento (“Foundation”)

A monolithic figure of rebar and concrete block. Cimiento does not dance; he observes. Known for standing perfectly still behind the visiting dugout for entire innings.

In The Stands: La Obra

The supporters’ section in right field wears hard hats and hi-vis vests. When Monterrey scores, they don’t cheer—they bang steel wrenches against the rail in a deafening industrial clatter.
