The Club

The Cocolos keep receipts — not in headlines, in rhythm. The park sits close to salt air and traffic, and the game carries the same pulse as the street: call-and-response, drums when the inning turns, laughter that isn’t mocking so much as inevitable.

Nothing here is “cute.” It’s festive, but not soft. It’s a working port that knows how to celebrate without asking permission.

  • Tradition: “La Moneda”
  • Stands: “El Malecón”
  • Local Rule: “No Quiet Innings”

Ballpark

El Malecón Park. A brick-and-concrete bowl with open corners that let the wind in. You can smell food before you see it. The lights come on early and the crowd doesn’t wait for permission to get loud.

Broadcast & Announcers

Broadcast

Marcos “Mar” Tejada. Play-by-play with the steadiness of tide charts—precise, unhurried, and allergic to drama.

In-Game Announcer

Lidia Santos. The voice inside the park. Lineups, calls, promotions—delivered like she’s conducting a block party, not reading a script.

Mascot & Stands

Coco

A human-worn costume with visible seams and scuffs—beloved because it’s been around. Coco doesn’t “perform.” Coco works the rail: high-fives, photos, kids on shoulders, constant motion.

El Malecón

Families, aunties, teenagers, dock workers, kids with ice cream. Drums. Coins on strings. Small flags. The noise isn’t a gimmick — it’s the default setting.
