The Club

The Frost don’t “bring energy.” They bring temperature. St. Paul plays a quiet, disciplined game that feels like breath on glass—controlled, steady, inevitable. They win by shrinking innings and stretching patience: long at-bats, clean defense, and pitchers who refuse to hurry. Opponents talk about the silence first. Then the strike zone.

  • Style: Control / Contact
  • Vibe: Cold Clarity
  • Tradition: “The Third-Inning Stand”

Ballpark

Frostbank Field. Built low and open to the river wind, the park is famous for its hard, bright light and the way sound carries in cold air. Fans say the foul lines feel closer here. The Frost say the same thing about the strike zone.

Broadcast

Len “Blue Band” Rask

Voice of the Frost. Rask calls the game like a weather report that learned baseball. Minimal, precise, dry humor. He’s obsessed with count leverage and defensive positioning, and he treats a 2–1 game like a cathedral.

Mascot & Stands

Rime

A padded, stitched figure in an old snowmobile suit with a fogged visor and a scarf that never stops moving. Rime doesn’t dance. Rime patrols.

The Quiet Deck

St. Paul’s fans have a thing they do: they stand together for the full third inning—no chants, no clapping, just presence. When the inning ends, they sit like nothing happened.
