The Club

Taipei plays under signage, streetlight, and late trains—baseball recorded in bright color without turning into a cartoon. The park feels alive even during warmups: vendor calls, camera shutters, and the soft click of cleats on concrete. Nothing here tries to be “mystical.” It just happens fast, and it looks good while it does.

Ballpark

Neon Harbor Park. A compact urban stadium boxed in by rooftops and LED boards. When the lights come on, the outfield fence glows like a shoreline. You can hear the game—bat on ball, glove pop, the crowd’s lift—over the city’s constant hum.

Broadcast & Announcers

Jia-Hao Chen

Play-by-play. Quick reads, clean pacing, and an ear for the crowd. Chen calls the game like he’s tracking motion—pitch, swing, contact—then lets the stadium do the rest.

Mei “Spark” Huang

In-stadium announcer. Bright, controlled energy with perfect timing—lineups, promos, walk-up cues. When she hits the last name, the crowd answers like it’s reflex.

Mascot & Stands

Glow

A human-worn mascot built for movement—soft edges, bright accents, and a costume that looks used, not manufactured for a photoshoot. Glow lives in the aisles: selfies, high-fives, and sprinting from section to section when a chant starts.

The Night Market Bleachers

Snacks, neon wristbands, handheld radios, and constant chatter. Fans keep score on their phones, then argue about the strike zone anyway. When a ball finds the gap, the entire section stands up at once—like a switch flipped.
