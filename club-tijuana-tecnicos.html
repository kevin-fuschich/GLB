The Club

The Técnicos don’t sell a myth or a “style of play.” They record what happens: quick hands, clean exchanges, and the small corrections that add up over nine innings. In Tijuana, the game feels close—fans right on the rail, voices carrying, and moments turning on details you almost miss.

Ballpark

Estadio de los Talleres. Concrete, steel, and bright signage with open sightlines down the lines. The park holds heat early and cools fast after sunset—lights buzzing on while the infield turns a deeper red.

Broadcast & Announcers

Isabel “Chispa” Rentería

Play-by-play. Fast reads, clean calls, and sharp resets between pitches. Rentería keeps pace without forcing it—letting the crowd do the heavy lifting when it swells.

Mateo Cruz

In-stadium announcer. A practiced voice built for hard surfaces—names, counts, and substitutions landing crisp. Promotions stay short. The baseball stays first.

Mascot & Stands

Torque

A human-worn mascot costume with visible seams and a little scuff—built to survive a season. Torque lives at the rail: quick high-fives, mock “inspections,” and photos with kids who show up early.

La Banda del Bullpen

Drums, hand claps, and constant chatter. People track every pitch like it matters. The cheer is immediate, the boos are creative, and the park never really goes quiet.
