A few things to know

Do I have to know baseball?

You can contribute to the world around the game: music, food, characters, fan rituals, businesses and more. Read the existing record and build from there.

What does a claim mean?

An accepted claim connects your User ID to the artifact and your published contribution. It gives you a place in the shared GLB record. Every submission goes through Board review.

Do I need a big idea?

A small detail, short story or character moment is a good start. For artwork or other supporting material, include a link in the contribution form.

What’s public?

Your accepted content and User ID appear on GLB. Your email and 16-character claim code stay private.

Give it your best shot

Help the Board see what you see.

You don’t need a polished deck. A clear idea with a little thought behind it goes a long way.

  1. Lead with the idea. Explain it in one or two sentences. What are you proposing?
  2. Find its place in GLB. Tell us where it belongs and what it adds. Read the existing record first; build on the world already here.
  3. Make it tangible. Include a sample scene, a character sketch, a rough visual or one specific example. Help us picture the thing.
  4. Leave room for other people. How could someone else contribute? The best starting points open more doors.
  5. Give it one more pass. Check names, explain unfamiliar references and make sure supporting links work. Keep baseball’s rules intact.
A simple pitch starter

“My idea is ___. It belongs in GLB because ___. Here’s what it could look like: ___. Other contributors could help by ___.”

Start with the strongest version of your idea. A focused paragraph and a useful example can do more than pages of background.
