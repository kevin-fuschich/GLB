Estadio de la Herradura

Santiago, Chile · Presented by TUMBAO

Horseshoe grandstands, open concourses and a mountain skyline that remains visible as the lights come on. The outfield spreads well beyond the dirt arc, with room for the corner outfielders to work toward the lines.

The venue keeps its established name: Estadio de la Herradura, presented by TUMBAO. The sponsor’s signs sit above the seats and along the outfield wall.

TUMBAO

Ropa y equipo

Gear for the trip and the return

TUMBAO makes outdoor clothing and equipment: weatherproof shells, packs, boots and blankets. Boxy cuts, useful pockets, reinforced seams and chunky hardware carry the same small woven label.

Aubergine, glacier blue, clay and acid yellow appear in deliberate panels. A broad black wordmark and angular stitched symbol identify the gear on a sleeve, a pack or a carrying strap.

Around the park

Something worth bringing back

Staff jackets and equipment bags put the gear to work at the stadium. A compact repair counter gives fans a place to bring a torn pocket, a loose strap or a stubborn buckle.

The products, the counter and the people who use them are starting points for contributors to help build the world around Santiago.

TUMBAO Blanket Night

Park giveaway · Selected home dates

Roll it up. Bring it back.

The stadium blanket has aubergine and glacier-blue panels, a yellow block and substantial edge stitching. A woven TUMBAO patch sits at the corner; stout straps and black buckles keep it rolled for the trip.

Unrolled across a lap, it stays useful as the air cools. The carrying strap leaves a hand free for a scorecard, a radio or the next thing passed down the row.

Dates and distribution details announced with the promotions schedule.

The TUMBAO repair counter

On the concourse

Keep the gear. Add to its story.

A small counter holds spare buckles, webbing, thread and a repair ledger. Fans bring gear that has been carried through seasons of games and trips outside the city.

A pack nobody wants replaced, a jacket whose owner is disputed, a blanket brought back for another patch: the counter is a place for contributors to introduce stories and connect them to existing artifacts. Its longer history remains unwritten.

Caballo

Club mascot

A horse-head suit with a soft grin and a slightly scuffed jacket. Caballo works the rail for high-fives and photographs, breaking into a short aisle sprint when a child points him out.

The name, look and role are established. Contributors can write the biography, history and encounters that give the character a fuller life.

La Tribuna

In the stands

Scarves, caps and handheld radios fill the rows. People stand when the count runs full. A close pitch brings a collective breath, then an argument that carries to the next batter.

Each supporter has a view of the game. Their stories and memories are open for contributors to develop.
