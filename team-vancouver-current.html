The Current

Club Identity

The Current are quieter than many GLB crowds until the game begins to turn. Then the sound arrives all at once. The club’s identity is coastal without becoming nautical costume: wet concrete, glass, transit, seawall traffic, reflected light, and a baseball park that feels connected to the city around it.

Game Voices

Vancouver Current · Broadcast & In-Stadium

Miles Harker

Play-by-play. Harker calls Vancouver games with a measured, low-hype cadence, leaving room for crowd noise, weather, and the long pauses that define a close game at WAP MOP SHOPPE STADIUM.

Gavin Cho

In-stadium announcer. Cho handles lineups, player introductions, scoring announcements, promotions, and the pacing between pitches. His voice belongs to the stadium, not the broadcast booth.

First PitchThe Low Tide

The stadium drops nearly to silence before the first pitch: no music bed, no prompt from the board, just the field and the room. The normal stadium sound returns after the pitch lands.

Late InningsThe Pull

When Vancouver brings the tying or winning run to the plate from the seventh inning on, the crowd rises without a scoreboard cue and stays standing through the plate appearance.

After a WinCurrent Out

The house lights stay low for a beat after the final out while a moving band of light circles the interior bowl once. No countdown. No chant. Then the park releases.
